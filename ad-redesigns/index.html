Concept work · Ad redesigns

Ad redesigns: five real ads, rethought

Before-and-after concept redesigns of five active UK Meta ads, from estate agents, a driving school, a plumber and a cleaning company.

I made and directed these concept designs myself using AI tools. For each ad I worked like I would on any brief: diagnose what's weak in the original, set one clear goal for the redesign, rewrite the copy using only claims the advertiser already makes, and explain why the new version should work better on a phone.

Unofficial spec redesigns: not affiliated with or endorsed by the advertisers.

These are unpaid portfolio exercises based on ads that are publicly visible in the Meta Ad Library. None of the advertisers commissioned them, no advertiser was contacted and none of the concepts has been published or run. No results are claimed. Advertiser logos are blurred in the "before" screenshots and aren't used in the redesigns.

Approach

The principles behind every redesign

1One message

One hook, one focal point and one offer, with minimal text on the image.

2A clear next step

The brand name is clearly placed, and the call to action says what happens next.

3Copy that scans

The first line works before "see more"; the rest is short and scannable.

For the video concept, I followed Meta's published guidance: brand and offer in the first 3 seconds, captions for sound-off viewing, and under 15 seconds. The points for the static images are general good practice, not Meta rules.

Concept 01 · Estate agent · valuation offer

Holden Estate Agents Unofficial concept

A busy patterned "Free online valuation" tile becomes one clear question, a simple house-and-£ illustration and a "Get my valuation" call to action.

Original ad
Image, active when captured from the Meta Ad Library on 9 Oct 2026 (running since 11 Mar 2026)
Library ID
991417203383178 (view the original ad in the Meta Ad Library, opens in a new tab)

1What's weak

  • White text sits directly on a busy topographic pattern, so contrast and phone-size legibility are weak.
  • The abstract image shows no home and no sign of the valuation tool, so the offer isn't visual.
  • The real hook ("What's your home worth?") is only in the headline under the image.
  • A generic "Learn more" button, with nothing saying the next step is a 60-second valuation.

Judged against general practice for static ads (one focal point, minimal on-image text, strong contrast, a clear call to action, mobile legibility), not a Meta rule.

2The new copy

First line
What's your home worth? Find out with a free instant online valuation in 60 seconds.
Headline
Free home valuation in 60 seconds
Button
Get quote (on-image cue: "Get my valuation →")

The rewritten copy only reuses claims already in the original ad.

3Why it's stronger

  • One focal point (house and £ gauge) on a calm gradient, so the white and yellow type has strong contrast at phone size.
  • The question hook, the "free · 60 seconds" offer and the brand are readable in the image itself, not only in the caption.
  • The call to action says what happens next, and the local areas (Maldon, Heybridge, Tiptree) sit right under the brand name.

Concept 02 · Estate agent · valuation offer

Oakmans Estate Agents Unofficial concept

A text-heavy yellow and black poster with a caption of about 180 words becomes one 60-second valuation promise, one proof chip and a short checklist caption.

Original ad
Image (one of several versions), active when captured from the Meta Ad Library on 9 Oct 2026 (running since 14 Mar 2025)
Library ID
1324989872073423 (view the original ad in the Meta Ad Library, opens in a new tab)

1What's weak

  • The image is a dense poster of tiny type, icons, ticks and crosses; at feed size almost none of it is readable.
  • The caption runs to about 180 words, and the valuation offer sits at the very end, below "see more".
  • A generic hook ("Selling or Letting? Choose Wisely!") instead of the benefit or the local postcodes.
  • No single offer or focal point, and a generic "Learn More" button.

Judged against general practice for static ads (one focal point, minimal on-image text, strong contrast, a clear call to action, mobile legibility), not a Meta rule.

2The new copy

First line
Selling or letting in B17, B29 or B31? Find out what your property's worth in under 60 seconds.
Headline
Your property's value in 60 seconds
Button
Get quote (on-image cue: "Value my home →")

The rewritten copy only reuses claims already in the original ad.

3Why it's stronger

  • One headline, one illustration and one proof chip ("No sale, no fee") replace a poster of small text.
  • The offer and postcodes are in the first line of copy, above "see more"; the strongest proof points from the original become three scannable ticks.
  • It keeps a yellow-on-black palette that echoes the original, but with large type and a clear call to action.

Concept 03 · Driving school · price offer

YM Driving Academy Unofficial concept

A gear-stick photo with the price hidden in the caption becomes "Automatic lessons from £28" as the hero, with a learner-car illustration and a clean description.

Original ad
Image, active when captured from the Meta Ad Library on 9 Oct 2026 (running since 1 Mar 2026)
Library ID
930101186200844 (view the original ad in the Meta Ad Library, opens in a new tab)

1What's weak

  • The strongest hook, "from only £28", is only in the caption; the image has no price.
  • A close-up gear-stick shot with no learner, instructor or L-plate doesn't read as "driving lessons" at a glance.
  • A logo box and contact panel crowd the right edge of the image.
  • The link description is a long keyword list that reads as spammy, and the button is a generic "Sign up".

Judged against general practice for static ads (one focal point, minimal on-image text, strong contrast, a clear call to action, mobile legibility), not a Meta rule.

2The new copy

First line
Automatic driving lessons in Leicester from only £28.
Headline
Automatic lessons from £28
Button
Book now (link description: "DVSA-approved instructors · Leicester")

The rewritten copy only reuses claims already in the original ad.

3Why it's stronger

  • The price becomes the visual hero, so the reason to click is visible without reading the caption.
  • A simple learner car with an L-plate says "driving lessons" instantly, with no stock photography.
  • A clean link description and a "Book now" button replace the keyword list and the vague "Sign up".

Concept 04 · Plumber · myth-busting message

High Tide Plumbing Unofficial concept

A headshot that doesn't match the message becomes a toilet-to-pipe "flushable" wipe illustration, with the myth-busting line on the image.

Original ad
Image, active when captured from the Meta Ad Library on 9 Oct 2026 (running since 28 Oct 2025)
Library ID
1183982653790471 (view the original ad in the Meta Ad Library, opens in a new tab)

1What's weak

  • The image (a man in a cap) doesn't illustrate the flushable-wipes myth, so image and message disconnect.
  • There's no text on the image; the "Flushable? Not quite." hook only appears in the caption and headline.
  • Vague link text, with no area named or reason to call now.
  • The "Call now" button appears greyed and low-contrast in the card.

Judged against general practice for static ads (one focal point, minimal on-image text, strong contrast, a clear call to action, mobile legibility), not a Meta rule.

2The new copy

First line
Plumbing myth: "flushable" wipes are safe for your pipes. Busted. 🚫
Headline
Flushable? Not quite.
Button
Call now

The rewritten copy only reuses claims already in the original ad.

3Why it's stronger

  • The illustration shows the myth (a "flushable" pack, a toilet and a blocked pipe), so the image and copy tell one story.
  • The hook is on the image in large type, so it works when someone is scrolling past.
  • A high-contrast "Call a local plumber" cue and a clear next step in the copy.

Concept 05 · Cleaning company · video ad

Brooklyn Cleaning Services Unofficial concept

An unbranded first frame and a caption of about 230 words become an 11.5-second 9:16 video with the brand and offer in the first frame, burned-in captions and a short grouped caption.

Original ad
Video, active when captured from the Meta Ad Library on 9 Oct 2026 (running since 20 Sep 2024)
Library ID
1047788896889402 (view the original ad in the Meta Ad Library, opens in a new tab)

▶Concept video (9:16, 11.5 s)

The redesigned video concept: the brand, hook and special offer on screen from 0 to 3 seconds, then kitchen, bathroom and every-room scenes, ending on an offer card. Captions are burned in on every frame, so it works with the sound off. It starts muted.

Unofficial concept: not affiliated with or endorsed by Brooklyn Cleaning Services.

1What's weak (judged against Meta's video guidance)

  • The brand isn't on the first frame. Meta's guidance is to capture attention quickly with brand imagery at the start and to feature the brand and key message within the first 3 seconds.
  • No captions are visible in the captured frame, although Meta advises designing for sound off.
  • The caption is about 230 words of bullets mixing three services, and the "special offer" only appears on screen, not in the copy.
  • A generic "Learn more" button.

Sources: Meta, Building video for mobile feed (opens in a new tab); Meta Business Help Center, Best practices for Instagram video ads (opens in a new tab).

2The new copy

First line
Deep clean, done for you. ✨ Find out the cost with our special offer.
Headline
Deep cleaning: see our special offer
Button
Get offer (end card cue: "Learn more →")

The rewritten copy only reuses claims already in the original ad.

3Why it's stronger

  • The brand name, the "Deep clean, done for you" hook and the special offer are all on screen from 0 to 3 seconds.
  • Burned-in captions on every frame make it work with the sound off.
  • At 11.5 seconds it stays under 15 seconds (Meta notes that feed videos under 15 seconds tend to perform best), and the 230-word list becomes three grouped bullets.
