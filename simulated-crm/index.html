Project Compass: a simulated CRM implementation

A complete set of project artefacts for a CRM roll-out at a fictional 25-person trades business, from business case to closure.

Simulated project: fictional company, created for portfolio and learning purposes.

Cobbleway Home Services Ltd is not a real business. The people (apart from me, in the project-manager role), dates, budget and results are invented and illustrative. I built this to practise the methods from my MSc modules. It is not client work, and none of the figures are real outcomes.

At a glance

The scenario

Cobbleway Home Services (fictional) is a plumbing, heating and electrical firm in North East England, with 14 field engineers, 2 apprentices and a 9-person office. Enquiries come in by phone, email and a website form, and are tracked in a shared inbox, two spreadsheets and a whiteboard. Quotes go unchased, service reminders depend on memory, and job details are typed in twice.

The project replaces this with a cloud CRM, configured by an implementation partner. The CRM is linked to email and the accounting package, gives engineers a mobile app, and brings in six years of cleansed customer data.

Approach

How the project was run

1Justify and set limits

An options appraisal (do nothing, improve spreadsheets, SaaS CRM, bespoke build) led to a charter with SMART objectives and PRINCE2-style tolerances: one week on time, contingency on cost, and Musts fixed on scope.

2Define and prioritise

Process-mapping workshops fed a MoSCoW catalogue with testable acceptance criteria. Stakeholders were scored on power and interest so engagement could be planned, not improvised.

3Plan the dependencies

A deliverable-based WBS became a dependency-driven schedule across eight phases, with UK bank holidays built in, a baseline, and milestones the sponsor signs off.

4Control and report

A weekly RAID review, change control (one approved change request) and a fortnightly one-page dashboard with calculated RAG ratings kept the sponsor informed.

5Manage the change

A champions network, a design role for the sceptical Field Supervisor, short hands-on training and two weeks of hypercare were aimed at the biggest benefit risk: adoption.

6Close and learn

The closure report compares baseline with actual, hands open risks and backlog items to a named business-as-usual owner, and sets dates for the benefits reviews.

What happened in the simulation

In week 8, the data audit found that around 18% of legacy customer records were duplicates (issue I01). Cleansing took 20 days instead of 10 and pushed the trial migration and UAT onto the critical path. The schedule showed the impact: go-live moved 4 working days, inside the one-week tolerance. At week 10 the dashboard reported Amber, with no exception needed. Separately, the accounting integration needed a paid connector, which was handled through change request CR01 (£900). The project closed £2,070 under the total budget, after using £1,430 of contingency.

Artefacts and downloads

Seven artefacts, one consistent set of numbers

Every document uses the same dates, names and budget, generated from a single data source so they can't drift apart. The workbooks use live formulas: change the status date on the dashboard and the milestone status, open-risk count and heatmap all recalculate.

01

Project charter and business case

Set out the problem, SMART objectives, scope, a four-option appraisal, costs, illustrative benefits, a three-year view and PRINCE2-style roles and tolerances (time ±1 week; cost within a £3,500 contingency).

How it was used: Approved at M1. The tolerances in this document later decided how the data-quality slip was handled: as a managed decision rather than a crisis.

02

Requirements (MoSCoW) and stakeholder register

18 requirements with acceptance criteria, prioritised Must / Should / Could / Won't. The stakeholder register scores power and interest, calculates a quadrant with a formula and records current versus desired engagement.

How it was used: The Must list fixed the scope tolerance. The register flagged the Field Supervisor as resistant, so he was given a design role early.

  • Excel for the Requirements (MoSCoW) and stakeholder register (XLSX, 13 KB)

03

WBS and schedule

A deliverable-based WBS (8 phases), a task list with durations, finish-to-start dependencies, effort, baseline and actual dates, a formula-driven weekly Gantt and a milestone plan. The CSV imports into Microsoft Project.

How it was used: Showed that the data-cleansing overrun moved onto the critical path, and by exactly how much: go-live +4 working days.

04

RAID log

Risks scored by probability × impact with a calculated rating, plus issues, assumptions, dependencies, a change log and the scoring key.

How it was used: Reviewed weekly. Two risks became issues (data quality, accounting connector), and one became change request CR01.

05

RACI matrix and communications plan

Thirteen activities across nine roles, with a formula check that each row has exactly one Accountable person, and a communications plan linked to the stakeholder quadrants.

How it was used: Made ownership clear for the hand-offs that usually go wrong: data rules, UAT sign-off and the go/no-go decision.

  • Excel for the RACI matrix and communications plan (XLSX, 10 KB)

Status dashboard (Excel)

A one-page highlight dashboard at week 10: calculated RAG by dimension, a milestone tracker, budget against actual and forecast, cumulative spend, a 5 × 5 risk heatmap and open issues. Everything runs on formulas and charts, driven by one status-date cell.

How it was used: Reported Amber honestly: schedule and cost were inside tolerance but forecast to vary, so the sponsor saw the slip early.

Closure report and lessons learned

Performance against each objective, baseline against actual for every milestone, cost by category, scope delivered, illustrative early benefit indicators, handover actions and six lessons learned.

How it was used: Closed the loop on the business case: what was delivered, what moved to the backlog and when the benefits will be reviewed.

What it demonstrates

What this practice project shows, and what it doesn't

  • Joined-up artefacts: the risk that occurred appears in the RAID log, the schedule, the dashboard and the closure report, with the same dates and figures each time.
  • Reporting against tolerances: RAG ratings are calculated from rules stated on the dashboard, not chosen by feel.
  • Excel for project control: NETWORKDAYS with a holiday calendar, COUNTIFS and SUMPRODUCT logic based on the status date, conditional formatting for the Gantt and heatmap, and native charts.
  • Methods from my MSc modules: Managing Projects with PRINCE2, Risk Management in Projects, Advanced Project Planning and Visualisation, and The Management of Change.

Limits, stated plainly

This is a simulation. There was no real sponsor, supplier or user group, and nobody pushed back the way real stakeholders do. The benefits and "early results" are illustrative. My delivery experience with real products is in the case studies.
